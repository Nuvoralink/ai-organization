# Sales Coaching Quality Lens

Load this reference before judging queued CoachAI call feedback.

## Core Standard

The coaching should help the rep improve the next call, not merely explain the current transcript. A good review names the highest-leverage behavior, proves it with grounded evidence, explains why it mattered, and gives a realistic replacement move.

## Communication Principles

- Respect plus control: the rep should be clear, calm, and direct without sounding pushy or vague.
- Active listening: acknowledge the prospect's concern before redirecting.
- Specificity beats reassurance: "I'll call you around 4" is weaker than a clear appointment, decision-maker plan, and purpose.
- Do not confuse politeness with skill. A friendly call can still fail if the rep never creates value, commitment, or clarity.
- Do not over-credit administrative cleanup. Email spelling, timezone checks, invite receipt, and phone-number logistics matter, but they rarely outrank unresolved purpose, commitment, decision-maker, objection, or cementing risk.

## Appointment Quality

Strong booked appointments usually include:

- Purpose: the prospect understands what the meeting is for and why it matters.
- Relevance/value: the meeting connects to the prospect's situation, concern, or benefit.
- Decision-maker clarity: spouse/partner/second decision-maker attendance is explicit when required.
- Concrete time control: the rep offers or confirms specific times without reopening vague availability.
- Cementing: confirm date/time, modality/location, attendee expectations, duration/window when required, and next step.
- Friction check: resolve "I'm at work," "not sure," "maybe," "I'll check," and similar uncertainty before treating the appointment as solid.

Weak booked appointments often contain:

- conditional attendance ("if my wife can make it," "I'll check and call back"),
- vague time ("maybe 4 PM"),
- hurry pressure that prevents purpose/cementing,
- unresolved legitimacy or "why are you calling me" resistance,
- prospect agreeing mainly to end the call,
- rep booking before clarifying the real issue or required participants.

## Objections And Resistance

First decide what should have happened strategically:

- objection recovery toward appointment,
- light timing continuation,
- legitimate callback fallback,
- controlled appointment booking,
- appointment cementing.

Then judge execution. Do not grade only the path the rep happened to choose.

Good objection recovery:

- acknowledges the concern,
- briefly reframes why the conversation or meeting is still useful,
- anchors to the prospect's context or approved material,
- asks a controlled next question or moves to a concrete appointment step.

Bad objection recovery:

- ignores the concern,
- apologizes and retreats without value,
- jumps to scheduling before rebuilding reason,
- argues,
- replaces the real sales issue with admin logistics.

## Discovery And SPIN

Discovery should uncover enough context to make the recommendation or appointment feel relevant.

- Situation questions gather facts.
- Problem questions surface pain, uncertainty, or gaps.
- Implication questions expand why the issue matters.
- Need-payoff questions help the prospect state value.

Do not punish clean, natural discovery just because it is not textbook wording. Do flag surface-level questions when the rep had a clear opening to understand the prospect's concern and skipped it.

## Call Control

Good control is not domination. It is clear navigation:

- set the frame,
- ask one question at a time,
- close loops,
- move from resistance to value to next step,
- keep the appointment concrete.

Red flags:

- prospect repeatedly says they are busy and the rep keeps expanding the call,
- rep lets the prospect control every next step without a clear commitment,
- rep confirms logistics after booking when the prerequisite should have come first,
- rep creates uncertainty with unnecessary windows or vague phrasing.

## No-Show Lens

When the known outcome is no-show, use it as a question, not a forced answer:

"What in the transcript made this appointment easy to miss?"

Common no-show drivers:

- unclear purpose,
- low perceived value,
- second decision-maker not committed,
- unresolved objection or legitimacy concern,
- weak or rushed cementing,
- conditional time/attendance,
- no controlled confirmation of what happens next.

Do not invent a no-show cause. If the transcript does not support one, say the artifact cannot prove it.

## Ranking Priority

Prefer the issue that most improves business outcome and rep behavior:

1. Unresolved objection, trust, legitimacy, decision-maker, commitment, or purpose gap.
2. Weak booking control or cementing that makes the appointment fragile.
3. Discovery miss that prevented relevance/value.
4. Delivery/tone issue that materially changed trust or cooperation.
5. Admin/logistics cleanup only when it materially affected outcome or caused confusion.

Secondary issues can be listed, but the primary fix should not be a lower-leverage cleanup when a stronger show-rate or trust issue is grounded.

## Replacement Line Standard

A suggested line should be:

- sayable by a real rep,
- grounded in transcript facts or approved material,
- matched to the selected issue,
- specific enough to practice,
- not a process instruction disguised as dialogue,
- not labeled as approved script unless it truly comes from approved material.

If strict sensitivity blocks generated examples, the coaching should still teach the behavior clearly without pretending a generated line is approved.

## Logical Consistency Checks

Fail the quality pass if:

- two different "primary" issues exist on different surfaces,
- UI/rep view promotes a different fix than the accepted primary decision,
- a final confirmation/admin issue outranks a stronger spouse/decision-maker/objection/purpose risk,
- the proof quote does not support the headline,
- the replacement line trains a different behavior than the selected issue,
- a good adaptive move is punished as script deviation,
- a material/script gap is blamed on the rep,
- a limited score blocks useful qualitative coaching when transcript evidence is safe,
- the final answer is polished but the rep would not know what to do differently.
